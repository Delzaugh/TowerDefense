# Systems Architect — replacement design brief

User direction, 2026-09-27: perform a creative, skeptical review; the current
model no longer reflects an application architect; re-imagine it. This supersedes
the original Pavilion form, including its roof and heavy cheek armour. Preserve
the friendly Copilot identity, palette family and compact limb-free anatomy.

## Critical review

- The literal tiered roof implies building architecture, not application design.
- Projecting cheeks and bulbous armour imply a protective helmet or tank.
- The role depends on a side-mounted icon, too small at gameplay distance.
- Added facets improve volume but do not explain the character's purpose.

## New design: the planning desk is the character's silhouette

A rounded, faceted teal body supports a large inclined planning desk. A narrow
cream frame surrounds the friendly screen. The top carries a raised software
architecture diagram: orange application window, cream service module and cream
data store, connected by a broad cyan route. These distinct components communicate
system decomposition and connections, not merely electronics or a building.
Staggered plan sheets emerge beneath the rear of the desk. One fitted horizontal
blueprint roll sits against the character-left side. No limbs, weapons or goggles.

Reference: references/systems_architect_concept.png, generated with the built-in
image tool from the saved prompt. It governs volume, inclined tabletop, narrow
bezel, rear sheet stack and scroll seating. Its generic square node caps are
adapted into application/service/data components to strengthen software meaning.
Original user illustrations remain historical palette/identity inputs.

## Landmarks and construction

Body width about 2.5m, base width 1.35m. Screen aperture around 1.7m wide and
1.2m high. Desk about 2.8m wide and 2.4m deep, front edge h=1.9m rising to
h=2.55m at rear. Main nodes project 0.15–0.3m from the desk. Total silhouette
approximately 3.15m wide including roll, 2.95m high, 2.6m deep. Floor at zero.
Screen remains modestly recessed; cheeks are trim, not the primary mass.
The crown diagram must be legible from the standard isometric camera. Rounded
body and changing cross-sections retain dimension from side/rear views.

Review primary front/side/isometric blockout before refining nodes and routes.
Inspect board/frame shared boundary, node seats, roll brackets and sheet roots
in close/reverse views. Check phone and small scale; the role must not rely on
text or tiny marks. Keep model and animation acceptance separate.
